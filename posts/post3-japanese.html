I learned Japanese without studying grammar

When I started learning Japanese I did it in the most traditional way possible: I bought a "How to learn Japanese" book and went through the entire thing. The vocabulary and reading parts were fun, but I absolutely dreaded the grammar parts. In fact, I hated studying grammar so much that I was about to give up on learning how to speak Japanese all together.

I told my mother about how I was feeling and she said something that made a lot of sense at the time: "If you don't like studying grammar, then just learn the things you do enjoy."

How I studied Japanese instead

Instead of focusing on grammar, I went all-in on vocabulary. My weapon of choice? Anki! Anki is the best spaced repetition application out there and it has helped me more than anything else when it comes to language learning.

Before I understood enough Japanese to watch anime without subtitles or read manga in Japanese, I would simply download huge Anki lists of Japanese words with their English meanings and spent an hour to sometimes even several hours memorizing these words. It wasn't fancy, it wasn't Instagram-worthy and it was nothing to brag about either... but it sure as hell was effective!

Once I reached a level where I was confortable enough to watch anime without subtitles and read manga in Japanese, I switched from pre-made Anki decks to sentence mining. Sentence mining means that you're learning new words through sentences you've heard either in real-life, read in a book or heard on tv. So let's say I hear the following sentence in Japanese: 犬が吠えていたので、眠れなかった。 Let's assume I know every word in this sentences except for the word 吠えていた. I'd add the entire sentence to my Anki deck and learn the word through context: I couldn't sleep because the dog was 吠えていた. Well through context one would assume 吠えていた would mean barking. Japan

Now although I don't need subtitles anymore when watching Japanese TV/Movies/Anime and I can read manga/comics in Japanese, I am far from fluent. The first time I'd notice this is when I went to Japan for the first time to do my minor, Globalization, there. Passively consuming and understanding content is very different from actively speaking a language and oh boy did that hit me like a truck!

Because I never bothered with grammar I had to learn how to speak Japanese the hard way: by making tons of emberassing mistakes while talking to people in Japanese. I have had people mock me and get mad at me plenty of times because I lacked the grammar to properly express myself.

I am still far from being fluent in Japanese, but I am able to hold normal conversations with native Japanese speakers nowadays and I am very proud of the progress that I've made so far. I don't think I'll ever reach true fluency like I did with English, but as long as I continue to visit Japan at least once a year and also continue sentence mining I am certain my Japanese will improve, albeit slowly.
